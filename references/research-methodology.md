# Targeted research methodology

## When to research
Use external research for external questions: competitors, current public pricing/features, market evidence, public regulations, or customer signals. Internal priorities, revenue, confidential roadmaps, and strategic choices belong to the company owner or authorized documents.

Do not browse merely to fill every template cell. With no browsing, record a research gap, sources needed, and a proposed query. Do not invent retrievals or imply memory is current research.

## Before retrieval
Define the claim to resolve, geography, period, audience, and useful evidence. Avoid sending confidential company text, customer lists, or internal figures to public search; use a generic query or ask when disclosure is required.

## Source protocol
Record source ID, title, publisher/owner, exact locator (URL, file/page, or conversation turn), source type, publication date if known, retrieval time, sensitivity, and limitations.

Prefer original product documentation, official data, direct company materials, and first-hand research. Secondary sources can contextualize but should not silently inherit primary authority. Multiple articles repeating one origin are not independent corroboration.

Use structured/unstructured and internal/external as source metadata. A spreadsheet is not more authoritative merely because it is structured; a founder statement is strong evidence of intent, weaker evidence of market demand.

## Claim extraction
Extract only the supported statement with a pinpoint locator. Record source.scope or record.scope when sample, geography, measurement, or period matters. Summarize rather than copying long third-party passages.

Public competitor prices are dated observations, not timeless truth. Reviews and comments are qualitative signals, not proof that a whole customer segment has a problem. Remove personal identities when not necessary.

For calculations, retain inputs and method in provenance/notes and mark the result's limits. Do not manufacture TAM/SAM/SOM from unsourced multipliers. Distinguish observation from inference.

## Conflicts
Check whether dates, segments, definitions, currencies, or product tiers explain a mismatch. If not, retain both records, link related_record_ids, and create a gap. Ask the relevant owner if internal; seek original or newer evidence if external. Do not select the more flattering claim.

## Stop
A normal pass uses up to five useful sources per question, adjusted to materiality and user time limits. Stop when adequate evidence is found or the remaining gap cannot be resolved with available access. Report limitations and next action; research volume is not a confidence score.
